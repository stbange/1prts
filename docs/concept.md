# 1stprts

The floor shakes when the first *Lintel* rolls out of the Motor Hall. From the inspection gantry you can see its driver waiting at the hatch. Beyond him, a plum-colored boat is climbing the flooded steps at Gate Six. Your infantry are still in the mustering yard. You can send them forward from here, ride with the tank, or stay and decide what the factory builds next. The boat keeps coming while you make up your mind.

## The war over the waterline

**Orra Basin, 2041.** An inland sea has receded through a chain of floodgates. At low water, causeways and old stocks of rolled steel emerge from the silt. Opening a gate can put those roads underwater again by morning. The Weir Assembly wants the basin low enough to connect its terrace towns by road. The Sounding Compact needs deep channels for the barges that feed its floating towns. At Gate Six, both can point to a settlement that will go hungry if the other side gets its way.

When the basin authority dissolved, its garrisons split. The terrace towns inherited the tracked causeway armor. The convoy yards took the landing craft and patrol aircraft. Both armies still use the authority's mill drawings and gate numbers. You can recognize a Plate Mill across the water before you can make out whose flag flies above it.

The pacing reference is the original 1995 *Command & Conquer*. A hauler returns, the mill pays out, a factory door opens and the unit you paid for joins a fight that is already moving. Here, every one of those events happens somewhere you can stand. The basin's water dispute supplies the campaign, and its dredges, gatehouses and bridge machinery supply the shapes of the armies. ## Being the commander

You are a field commander attached to an Assembly yard. Your authority travels by radio. Your body travels on foot or aboard something a crew operates. The tank commander runs the vehicle while you lean out of its cupola and give orders to the rest of the column. In later operations a pilot can fly you to a site and an engineer can keep a ship running while you are on another deck.

Three activities form the strategy loop.

1. **Read the field.** Look through the gate gallery windows, ask a scout for a report, or bring up the route diagram on your slate. A remote contact carries its observer's call sign and report time. From the tank hatch you can check the approach for yourself.
2. **Give an order.** Choose a crew or group by sight or call sign, choose a job, then confirm a visible target or reported location. From the same slate, mark a surveyed pad for construction, queue a tank or tell a section to take the switch room. Each crew acknowledges, explains a blockage and calls in completion. Radio calls have captions and enter a log.
3. **Choose where to be next.** Ride to the gate for a view of the flooded lane, or stay at the mill to see whether stock is still sitting in a crawler. Your crews keep moving, building and fighting while you travel.

The slate in your hand and the chart at the command desk show schematic symbols, routes and report ages. Lift your eyes from either and you see the room around you. Construction placement, production, group orders, boarding, combat and the debrief all keep the view at the commander's eyes. In a headset, that includes the natural separation of the two eyes.

## What makes it an RTS

A *Mole* salvage crawler makes a round trip between an exposed steel yard and a staffed Plate Mill. Raw plate becomes **alloy** when the mill processes the load. The ledger shows cargo in transit, available alloy and reservations separately. If both sides reach the same pile, every load taken leaves one fewer for the other. With a long trip to the next yard, spending the first loads well matters.

The Survey Hall takes a plan and a surveyed footprint. Fitters build it, its assigned crew reports for duty, and the new facility opens for orders. A Dynamo House supplies **power**. A Watch House musters infantry. A Motor Hall produces crewed vehicles. You can set the priority of a factory on the grid. Open its door and you can see which hull is halfway through inspection when the lights go out.

Groups accept move, scout, escort, engage, hold and withdraw orders as their roles permit. Infantry can occupy a switch room. Armor can contest the approach and drive opposing units from the yard. A disabled vehicle keeps its crew and interior and can be recovered. A standing order to escort the salvage crawler keeps it safer but commits a unit you might need at the gate. Both faction commanders pay for their forces and act on what their crews report.

The two sides grow differently. The Assembly can invest surplus power in a fixed yard and keep a priority production job running during a shortage. A Compact yard can tow a floating service building to a new bank. Its amphibious carriers can bring infantry to a gate along a channel an Assembly tank cannot cross.

## A world built at crew scale

One world unit is one metre. A roughly 1.7 m standing eye height, a 2.1 m personnel doorway, railings, numbered bulkheads and people on watch give you a ruler everywhere. Large assets earn their size through what fits inside them. A player should be able to park *Mole* on the ferry, climb above it and look back down at the same vehicle.

| Asset family | Example scale | A route the commander can walk | Who works there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Building | 65 m shore depot | Loading apron → dispatch office → gantry over the bay | Dispatcher and loading crew |
| Ground vehicle | 12 m causeway tank | Rear hatch → radio station → driver or cupola | Driver, radio operator and tank crew |
| Ship | 96 m roll-on ferry | Bow ramp → vehicle deck → stair tower → bridge | Pilot, deckhand and engineer |
| Aircraft | 44 m cargo tiltrotor | Tail ramp → cargo hold → flight deck | Pilot and cargo chief |
| Spacecraft | 85 m orbital tender | Docking collar → pressure lobby → habitat → flight deck | Flight crew and systems engineer |
| Space station | 800 m across its main truss | Arrival collar → transfer hall → lift → traffic control | Dock crews and watch officers |

Every building and craft in play has people on watch, a human-scale entrance and connected spaces to explore. When the rival ferry ties up, you can board it and meet a crew answering to another skipper. A landing ramp, airlock or docking collar connects actual walkable surfaces. From a moving bridge you see the quay slide away while the deckhand beside you can keep one hand on the rail. Ladders and lifts shorten trips between decks, and the radio lets you work on the way. Machinery occupies solid space on the plans. The spaces around it have room for people to do their jobs.

## First playable mission · Gate Six

You begin in the Assembly Survey Hall. Through its open door you can see *Mole* waiting on a concrete pad. Across the gate, the Compact has its own hall, hauler and yard crew. The neutral gatekeeper calls in from the pumps while your dispatcher marks the rival's last sighting on the wall chart in front of you.

**Build a force.** Each side starts with eight alloy, a working Dynamo House, fitters and a reserve of operators. Commission a Plate Mill for four alloy, then choose when to build the Watch House (three) and the Motor Hall (six). A full processed haul pays four. You can muster a two-alloy Gate Section after one haul on the infantry opening. Saving for a four-alloy *Lintel* first gives you a way to contest the yard early, but you still need a section to take the room.

**Fight over a place you can walk through.** The Assembly tank can hold the dry causeway. The Compact's *Wake* can land a Channel Section at the waterside door. The opposing commander sends its own hauler, queues its own forces and can win if you stay in the factory. From a hatch you might see the landing immediately. At the Survey Hall you have to decide whether your last report from the bank is current enough to move the guard.

**Win condition.** The first side with a line section in the switch room and an uncontested yard for 60 consecutive seconds controls Gate Six. An opposing line section in the room or operational enemy armor in the painted yard interrupts the claim and resets its clock. The radio reports who interrupted and where. Your body can be in the factory, in a tank, or on the stairs beside your section when the decision comes through.

## Second mission · Tide Window

At the public port, the two sides honor a berth schedule so civilian ferries can still land. You command an Assembly supply crossing. The Compact claims its scheduled slot, and the neutral island relay needs power before high tide.

**Win condition.** Bring two power cores from the mainland depot to the island relay. Its resident technicians must finish installing both and bring the relay online before high tide at 12:00 of simulation time. The clock starts after the briefing. If the tide turns first, the island crew reports the missed window.

**Starting position.** You stand at the depot with eight alloy, two cores, one fitting crew, *Mole* and *Kestrel*. Both vehicles have their own operating crew. The crawler can load the cores one at a time. The island has a public quay, a gravel slip and a staffed relay hall. A rival crewed ferry uses the public quay from 05:00 up to 08:00. The approach buoy broadcasts its reservation. Watch a clock and you can plan around it.

| Use of the fitting crew | What it buys | What you give up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spend six alloy to commission the depot's quay hoist | The crew brings the hoist cab online and loads both cores in one lift. Sail early for the public quay, or wait for it to clear. | The standard ferry ramp requires the public quay. A late arrival costs time. |
| Spend six alloy to fit an extended bow ramp to *Kestrel* | The pilot can land at the gravel slip during the rival's reservation. | *Mole* makes both loading trips, and the island haul from the slip is longer. |

You have enough alloy for one improvement. On either route *Mole* crosses aboard *Kestrel*, disembarks and delivers the cores. The island technicians handle installation. You can ride in the crawler, walk out onto the ferry's deck, go up to the bridge for the approach, or direct the whole trip from shore. If a berth is occupied the pilot holds, tells you why, and accepts a new destination compatible with the fitted ramp. A running ferry, a queued crew and the tide clock keep moving while you consult the chart.

A frugal commander can also keep all eight alloy and wait for the 08:00 public slot. With a prompt discharge, there is one minute to spare. The debrief records the relay's activation time and remaining alloy so the fastest crossing and the cheapest crossing each have a payoff.

The port mission succeeds if both investments can win, a missed tide produces a legible loss, and you can explain a delay from something you saw or a crew told you.

## Art and sound

From the gate gallery, pick out the Assembly by high square cabs and paired gauge marks. The Compact's plum-colored carriers have low bows and offset deckhouses. Use low-polygon 3D geometry for silhouettes, rooms, stairs, ramps and everything you can walk on. Use small paletted textures, mipmaps and directional sprites for distant infantry, spray and work signals. Nearby crew have visible work poses at their stations. Optional voxels suit chunks of breakwater stone or cargo dressing.

The change from an open quay to a vehicle deck should be audible. Wind gives way to a hollow ramp impact and machinery through a bulkhead. Radio lines start with the call sign. Lamps pair color with a shape or label. World textures can be coarse while chart text, captions and control labels stay legible at reading distance.

## Campaign progression

| Stage | New decision | Playable proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground | Muster infantry early or spend the first loads on armor | Build a powered base and take *Gate Six* through ordinary field orders. The rival can win by the same rule. |
| Shore and sea | Buy throughput or access to another landing | Complete *Tide Window* from aboard the ferry and from shore. |
| Compact operations | Move the floating repair yard upriver or keep it servicing the convoy | Begin a separate operation aboard the Slip Shed as its Compact commander. Land a Channel Section from a *Wake* and recommission the upstream mooring. |
| Air | Assign a patrol flight to the convoy or commit the airfield crew to the next transport | Board a staffed aircraft, walk to the flight deck in flight, then direct a handoff to the ground crew. |
| Orbit | Allocate a tender and a station berth while surface crews continue working | Ride the tender, cross its collar into the station, and receive a surface crew's progress report from traffic control. |

The first playtest should answer two questions. Can a new commander trace a late tank back to the mill, the power meter or the factory queue from where they stand? Does boarding the first tank give them a view of the gate worth leaving the factory for?
