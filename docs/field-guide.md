# Orra Basin field guide

## Who is fighting

| Field note | Weir Assembly | Sounding Compact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constituency | Towns built on the dry terraces. They elect gatekeepers to keep the lower causeways open. | Families, yards and towns that move freight by water. Their crews elect a skipper for each convoy. |
| What winning means | A continuous road and power grid gives isolated towns a winter supply route. | A navigable channel keeps the basin's laden barges moving between settlements. |
| How a base grows | Investment stays at the yard. An Accumulator Gallery can keep one priority job running through a power shortage. | Investment can follow the fleet. A Slip Shed can move a repair berth to another surveyed bank. |
| Read at a distance | Square roofs, high cabs, paired white gauge bars on petrol-blue panels. | Low boat bows, offset deckhouses, a cream draft circle on plum panels. |

Both sides employ mechanics, infantry, drivers and boat crews. Their starting resource and production rules are shared. Their ground forces already have different options at the first gate.

## Forces

The dimensions below are proposed hull lengths. Each vehicle includes a crewed operating station and a second accessible work area. Troops embark and disembark as people.

| Unit | Faction and role | What the commander can do or see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fitting crew | Shared engineers. Build on surveyed pads and return disabled vehicles to service. | Meet the foreperson at the work face and assign the next job by call sign. |
| *Mole*, 22 m | Shared tracked salvage hauler. Cycles between exposed plate and a powered mill. Can be reassigned to carry freight. | Enter the cutter control room from the rear passage, then walk forward to the driver's cab. The load dial shows cargo still aboard. |
| Gate Section | Assembly line infantry. Occupies control rooms and can hold a gate after its armor moves on. | Walk alongside the section leader or give the same order from a tank. See the watch physically enter the building. |
| *Lintel*, 12 m | Assembly causeway tank. Strong in a prolonged contest on a dry yard. The low channel route is impassable to it. | Board through the rear hatch and pass the radio operator on the way to the driver's station. From the cupola you can see the infantry's progress. |
| *Plumb*, 11 m | Assembly survey car. Parks at a benchmark to refresh reports on an adjacent sector. Moving it opens another field of view and gives up the first. | Step from the map bench to the raised observation cabin and sight the benchmark yourself. |
| *Span*, 15 m | Assembly engineering carrier. Carries a section and opens a marked crossing while committed to that site. | Walk from the troop benches to the bridge operator's booth. An occupied crossing ties up the carrier. |
| *Tally*, 14 m | Assembly mobile artillery. A support order commits it to one reported yard. Stale reports can leave it supporting an empty objective. | From the rear entrance you can reach the plotting booth and see the sector tag the crew is working from. |
| Channel Section | Compact line infantry. Can land from a *Wake* and take a control room on foot. | Ride in the same troop bay and watch the section file out. Its leader retains command when you stay aboard. |
| *Wake*, 15 m | Compact amphibious armored carrier. Takes one section through a surveyed shallow channel. Its crew yields sooner than a *Lintel* in a sustained yard contest. | Walk from the wheelhouse into the troop bay. Read the draft marks through a side window as the crew changes from water to land. |
| *Reed*, 13 m | Compact scout launch. Supplies current reports along wet channels. Its deep keel restricts it to water. | Step from the chart cabin onto the sounding deck to see what the lookout can see. |
| *Hinge*, 18 m | Compact recovery tractor. Can tow a disabled hull or relocate a Slip Shed. Each tow ties up the tractor. | Visit the driver's station and the tow supervisor's enclosed rear platform. |
| *Crossbill*, 24 m | Compact patrol aircraft. Screens one group on a timed sortie, then needs a staffed Flight Shed before it can launch again. | Enter behind the flight deck and visit the observer's cabin. Its tally board shows how much of the patrol is left. |
| *Kestrel*, 96 m | Basin roll-on ferry. Carries vehicles and freight between ports. Both sides operate examples of this civilian hull. | Board by the vehicle ramp, leave a parked crawler, climb the stair tower and stand watch on the bridge. |

The first operation fields fitters, haulers, both infantry types, the *Lintel* and the *Wake*. Scouts, specialist vehicles and faction facilities enter in later operations. *Tide Window* introduces the ferry.

## The base at walking height

| Building | What it contributes | Route to the crew |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Survey Hall | Command post. Surveyed pads, group orders and the production ledger are available at its wall chart and on your carried slate. | Surveyor's porch → dispatch room → chart gallery. A clerk moves a numbered tag when a crew accepts a job. |
| Dynamo House | Six units of grid capacity per staffed house. Crews report which queues are waiting for power. | Generator floor → switch room. The meter row uses the same building numbers as the slate. |
| Plate Mill | Processes each full crawler load into four spendable alloy. An idle or unpowered mill gives you no income. | Weighbridge → sorting floor → batch office. You can watch a load arrive before the alloy appears on the ledger. |
| Watch House | Mustering hall for infantry. A trained section forms at the door and reports for orders. | Bunk room → assembly floor → yard exit. You can follow the group into its transport. |
| Motor Hall | Vehicle factory. It produces the owner's tank or carrier once the mill is commissioned and the hall is staffed and powered. | Fitting floor → inspection gantry → vehicle door. Meet the driver of the vehicle you just ordered. |
| Yard Post | Crewed static defense. Holds its marked approach while operational. Its effect ends at that yard. | Duty room → stair → armored lookout. Its reports identify which yard is under pressure. |
| Flight Shed | Airfield service and production building. The same ground crew readies patrol flights and receives cargo aircraft, so an incoming delivery can delay the next sortie. | Hangar apron → service floor → flight office with a strip board for the next arrivals. |
| Accumulator Gallery | Assembly facility. With a charged reserve a player-designated bay can finish one job during a power shortfall. Recharges on surplus grid power. | Cell aisle → duty desk → priority board. The operator chalks the bay number on the board. |
| Slip Shed | Compact floating service works. Repairs a vehicle at a mooring. A *Hinge* can tow the shed to a surveyed bank, with work suspended until moored and staffed again. | Pontoon gangway → repair cradle → dispatch loft. The crew rides with the building. |
| Gate Six station | Shared objective. The neutral gatekeeper runs the machinery. A faction's watch can take control of the switch room. | Waterside door → pump gallery → switch room overlooking the painted yard. |

## Beyond the ground war

A 44 m cargo tiltrotor shares the Flight Shed apron with the patrol aircraft. The spaceport services an 85 m tender through a pressure lobby. Aboard it you can walk from the transfer hold to the flight deck. The orbital station's 800 m main truss links the docking collars to traffic control by lift. The same call signs, numbered hatches and crew reports carry the commander from the yard to orbit.

## Visual and audio direction

Build the armies from original studies of floodgates, plate rollers, bridge tractors and survey launches. Carry their shapes into low-poly models, hand-painted markings and directional sprites. From the gate gallery, a *Wake* should read by its bow and offset deckhouse through the mist. A *Lintel* shows a high square cupola above the yard wall. Both stay recognizable in grayscale.

The Assembly shift bell sounds two short strokes. A Compact skipper signals departure with a low ferry horn. A mill operator might call, “Batch four weighed. You can spend it.” Briefings use a gatekeeper's grease-pencil waterline chart in the room with the commander. Create the insignia, maps, radio performances, score and interface artwork for these people and places.
